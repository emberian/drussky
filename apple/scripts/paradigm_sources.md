# Sources for `paradigms.json`

`build_paradigms.py` writes Russian inflection as the course needs it: three noun declensions,
adjectives, personal and possessive pronouns, the two conjugations with the irregular verbs the
course uses, the past tense, the imperative, and the case uses taught in the course. Nothing in
it is a new analysis. A word is authored as its lemma, gender, animacy and stress pattern; the
generator applies the standard ending tables and spelling rules, and `validate_paradigms.py`
checks the result against tables copied by hand from the references below. Where a word steps
outside the rules (стул → сту́лья, челове́к → лю́ди, мать → ма́тери), its cells are written out
in the author file and the learner meets them as words; the `exceptions` list names the forms in
the course that no table produces.

## References

- **Wiktionary**, Russian entries (en.wiktionary.org). Every lemma's declension or conjugation
  table there (cited to Zaliznyak) is the reference for the forms, stress included. The thirty
  noun, three adjective, thirteen verb and three pronoun tables in `validate_paradigms.py`
  (`REFERENCE_*`) are copied from these pages; every other word in the file is generated by the
  same rules from its Zaliznyak letter and checked for shape.
- **А. А. Зализняк, Грамматический словарь русского языка** (1977; 4th ed. 2003) — the
  declension classes and stress patterns. The author file names each word's pattern by his
  letter: *a* stem stress throughout; *b* ending stress throughout; *c* stem in the singular,
  ending in the plural (го́род, города́); *d* the reverse (окно́, о́кна); *d′* as *d* with the
  accusative singular on the stem (вода́, во́ду); *e* stem except the oblique plural (ночь,
  ноче́й). Fleeting vowels (оте́ц, отца́; день, дня) and the plural stems with ё (звезда́,
  звёзды) are written as stem overrides.
- **Terence Wade, A Comprehensive Russian Grammar** (3rd ed., Wiley-Blackwell 2011), chapters
  on the noun (§§ 44–74), adjective (§§ 143–160), pronoun (§§ 110–142) and verb (§§ 212–245) —
  the ending tables, the spelling rules (ы → и after к г х ж ш ч щ; unstressed о → е after
  ж ш ч щ ц; я → а, ю → у after hushers; a stressed е of a soft ending written ё), the inserted
  vowel of the genitive plural (о after a velar or before к, е/ё elsewhere, with ь and й giving
  way: ло́жек, о́кон, пи́сем, сестёр), the animate accusative, and the imperative rule (vowel
  stem → -й; consonant stem → -и when the first person is end-stressed, else -ь).
- **Русская грамматика** (АН СССР, 1980), vol. I §§ 1173–1230 (noun declension and stress
  schemes) and §§ 1551–1575 (verb classes) — the account of the mixed conjugation of хоте́ть
  and the athematic verbs есть and дать.
- The course's own uses of the cases (`uses` in the file: у меня́ есть, в/на + prepositional,
  the accusative object and в/на + accusative, the dative receiver, с + instrumental, times of
  day, нет + genitive, the genitive after numbers) follow the sequence in which the course
  introduces them (`build_course.py`), with their rules phrased as in Wade §§ 86–108.

## Policy

1. **The generator is the rule; the references are the check.** A rule the generator cannot
   express is not patched into the rule — the word gets an `over=` cell and, if the course uses
   the form, an entry in `exceptions` with the one-line reason a learner needs.
2. **Stress is marked as the course marks it** (`COURSE_SCHEMA.md` rule 2): U+0301 on the
   stressed vowel of a word of two or more syllables, never on ё, never on a monosyllable. The
   adjective's animate accusative is written as both forms, `но́вый / но́вого`.
3. **Word choice follows the course.** Every declinable or conjugable lexicon word is in the
   file; the few extras (ру́сский, кото́рый, дать, мочь, смотре́ть) are there because the
   course's phrases or its stated aims (мочь, дать among the irregulars) need them.
4. **Proper nouns and mass nouns are singular only** (Земля́, Луна́, молоко́, са́хар, пра́вда):
   the plural cells exist in the table, not in these words.
5. **Cells share components.** A cell names the systematic component (`gram:decl1-gen-sg`) and
   the course's older, coarser pattern it also exemplifies (`gram:genitive`), so evidence from
   pages written before the tables still fills them. Pronoun and irregular-verb cells are the
   forms themselves (`form:меня`), since there the word is the pattern.
