## Recommendation

Use Claude as the teacher, local full-precision Qwen3-ASR as its ears for words, local alignment for replay navigation, and measured acoustic features for narrowly defined observations. Add an audio-capable Gemini listener for **tentative, time-localized listening hypotheses**, independently of the text teacher. Teach immediately through demonstration, meaningful contrasts, replay, and self-repair; improved calibration can progressively enable stronger diagnoses. A novice should not have to supply a labeled corpus before learning. Ember's friend remains an optional breakfast/dinner conversation partner, with no recording, labeling, approval, or coaching obligation.

Choose the speech generator by the Russian it actually produces for the current teaching target. Model size, BF16 precision and a multilingual model card are useful candidates for evaluation, not a quality ranking. The first local sample already illustrates why: most content was recoverable, while the specially stressed homographs triggered anomalous recognition. Current `local_russian` routing sends authored stress to Silero and suitable contextual ordinary text to Qwen, with a declared short-input strategy; it does not silently substitute a failed backend. Milena remains a separately selectable system voice. A difficult reference can receive a bounded alternative candidate without blocking everyday learning or requiring a human label.

The near-term useful loop is: hear a short meaningful example → identify its meaning/contrast → imitate or answer → hear one localized observation or tentative correction → replay reference and attempt → make one new attempt → use the distinction in a new phrase later. Keep the message and gloss visible. Offer an explanation without a production demand when working memory is occupied. A native-like accent remains a legitimate long-term aspiration; do not replace it with a single machine nativeness score.

## What Russian evidence changes in the product

**Stress is a lexical choice and an acoustic realization.** Store the intended sense, inflected form and stressed vowel explicitly in teaching material: `за́мок` and `замо́к` have the same ordinary spelling. A transcript that says `замок` cannot settle which was pronounced. Do not map highest F0 to stress. Chrabaszcz et al. manipulated cues in **English** nonwords for English, Mandarin and Russian listeners; Russian listeners relied strongly on vowel quality and minimally on pitch. This supports caution about cue transfer, not a Russian stress classifier. [Chrabaszcz et al. (2014)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5503100/).

**Reduction depends on context.** In Russian read speech, prosodic prominence and phrase-final lengthening affect vowel reduction; duration reduction and quality reduction need not occur together. A fixed unstressed/stressed duration ratio or a universal F1/F2 target would overcorrect legitimate productions. Teach ordinary-speed examples in a phrase, show the stressed vowel, then compare the same word in another phrase. Use independently aligned vowel intervals and speaker/context normalization before giving measurement-based stress suggestions. [Kocharov, Kachkovskaia and Skrelin (2019)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2019/kocharov19_interspeech.pdf).

**Palatalization is a meaning-bearing category, not just a visual shape.** Simonchyk and Darcy studied 40 American learners and 10 Russian speakers with ABX discrimination and auditory word-picture matching. Better perception did not guarantee separate lexical representations; word-final contrasts were especially problematic. Pair sound identification with meaning, then production and retrieval in a new context. The adjacent vowel's formant transitions and release carry context-dependent cues; a whole-word spectrogram distance or a single F2 cutoff is not a palatalization detector. Start with clear contrasts and teach a tongue-body gesture as an instruction; never claim to have measured the learner's tongue position from audio. [Simonchyk and Darcy (2017)](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/c21525f1-31d0-4bd8-ac79-d571d5f18f29/content).

**Fluency is task-dependent temporal organization.** Speech rate, articulation rate, pause location, and repair behavior describe different things. Fontan et al. found useful relationships between low-level acoustic measures and expert ratings in Japanese learners of French; that does not supply Russian cutoffs. Show a localized long pause as an observation, then offer a phrase chunk. Do not punish a thoughtful pause in a philosophy answer or equate speaking faster with improvement. Compare later performances of a similar task, separating rehearsal from spontaneous use. [Fontan et al. (2018)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2018/fontan18_interspeech.pdf).

**Intelligibility, ease of understanding, and accent are distinct outcomes.** Keep message comprehension, sound contrast, and prosodic naturalness separately observable. A listener or recognizer recovering the intended word does not demonstrate a native-like realization. Conversely, a conspicuous accent need not block meaning. [Munro and Derwing's experimental work (1995)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002383099503800305) motivates separate task measures; its English findings do not establish Russian scoring thresholds.

## Error boundaries that determine behavior

1. **Recognizers often repair speech.** Correct output can reflect language-model expectations; incorrect output can reflect ASR failure. Run the first transcript without the expected phrase, homograph sense, learner nationality, or a list of likely mistakes. Preserve that output before any target-conditioned pass. Agreement between two recognizers is useful evidence of lexical recoverability, not independent human intelligibility or phonetic correctness.
2. **Forced alignment assumes its text.** Align the independently hypothesized/confirmed transcript for replay. Aligning the lesson target can make omitted or substituted words appear to have timestamps. Preserve which text was supplied and whether an alignment was target-conditioned. Qwen's 80 ms grid and pseudo-label training specifically rule out treating its word boundaries as precise phone boundaries. [Qwen technical report, §3.3–3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21337).
3. **Better ASR is not automatically better assessment.** Shi et al. experimentally found that changing the proportion of nonnative speech in acoustic-model training affects alignment and pronunciation scoring differently. A model trained to accommodate an accent can be a good transcriber while hiding a distinction the teacher wants to help establish. [Shi et al. (2023)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/shi23_interspeech.pdf).
4. **Novice speech violates assumptions of tidy phone errors.** Robertson et al.'s beginner-French study found whole-syllable/word problems and limited expert agreement. Use a word or short phrase as the repair unit when a phone diagnosis would be spurious or cognitively unhelpful. Their numerical agreement is not a Rufren accuracy estimate. [Robertson et al. (2016)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2016/robertson16_interspeech.html).
5. **Audio LLM explanations can be plausible but unsupported.** A recent L2-English preprint tests evidence conditions and reports stereotyped diagnoses and a gap between coherent explanations and correct labels. Treat it as a useful adversarial-evaluation design, not proof about every current model or Russian. Test the same audio with altered target text, omitted demographic hints, no audio, and swapped audio. A diagnosis that follows the supplied stereotype instead of the recording must be downgraded. [Wang and Sun (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15325). Another zero-shot assessment study concerns L2 English and broad rubric agreement; neither study validates phone-level Russian feedback here. [Parikh et al. (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16230).

These boundaries should produce helpful actions, not repeated refusal messages. For example: “I heard **был**; you meant **бил**. Let's compare these two words, then try **Он бил** once.” Until independently supported, phrase this as a listening hypothesis rather than “Your ы was wrong.” If the recognizers disagree, “Let's hear this word again” with two labeled replay choices is better than a guessed diagnosis. If both agree with the target, reinforce the successful message and choose a fresh transfer phrase; do not certify every sound.

## Concrete processing and teaching contract

The implemented first stage stores `SpeechTranscription` through `Fact::SpeechTranscribed`, bound to the exact immutable recording attachment, attempt, revision, asset hash, original-frame span, source format, model/runtime provenance and bounded worker audit JSON. `TranscriptConfirmation` separately records confirm/edit and supersession; `AiAssessment.confirmed_transcript` binds language feedback to the adopted words. These are core/sync v3 facts. Do not reinterpret this confirmed text as evidence of acoustic correctness.

A richer future `SpeechEvidenceBundle` can add signal measurements, alignments and direct-listener hypotheses while retaining those bindings. It should not widen text-only `AiTextEvidence` or bypass `AiAssessment::validate`. Keep raw audio-model output for review and validate the structured result before it reaches the teacher.

Every bundle binds `request_id`, `attempt_id`, `revision_id`, `context_id`, exact original recording asset ID + SHA-256, canonical PCM SHA-256, frame count/rate, and optional reference asset/revision. Original capture remains untouched. Each derivative records its parent hash, original-frame start/end, resampling/mixdown/version, and sample-rate mapping. Crop times are half-open original-frame intervals; all downstream timestamps carry their coordinate system. Validate containment, ordering, finite values and duration bounds. A stale result for another attempt/crop cannot attach.

Evidence entries have distinct kinds:

- `transcript_hypothesis`: literal transcript, language, unprompted/target-conditioned mode, model/runtime revisions and decoding settings. Optional model scores are raw provider scores, never a generic probability of correctness. Keep alternative words when the provider actually emits them; do not invent confidence values.
- `word_alignment`: text supplied, source transcript ID, approximate intervals, algorithm/revision, `resolution_ms`, and `purpose: replay_navigation`. No implied phone labels or correctness verdict.
- `signal_observation`: measurement type, units, original-frame span, method/version, quality flags and validity. Initial types are already-supported clipping/level/periodicity/contour observations. Do not rename quiet blocks as VAD, missing F0 as wrong intonation, or pitch confidence as speech-recognition confidence.
- `listener_hypothesis`: audio-capable model, exact audio hash, heard alternative, target dimension, supporting span, alternative explanation and an explicit uncertainty state. Each hypothesis must request a checkable teaching action. Uncalibrated model self-confidence is not a calibrated probability.
- `lexical_target`: source text, intended meaning, stress mark, target contrast, acceptable variants and provenance. This is curricular knowledge, never an observation about the recording.

The feedback policy emits **one primary action** plus optional detail:

| Evidence situation | Teaching action | What is recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No usable audio / silence / severe clipping | Short capture instruction and immediate retry | Recording-quality observation; no language failure |
| Transcript differs; learner confirms intended message | Replay word in context, give contrast, invite self-repair | Lexical listening hypothesis and subsequent learner confirmation |
| Recognizers disagree or target-alignment questionable | Replay a padded phrase span; offer “I meant…” alternatives | Unresolved ambiguity; do not pick a fake winning probability |
| Direct listener suggests stress difference, uncalibrated | Show stressed spellings/meaning, play both references, ask which matches, retry | Tentative sound hypothesis; listening discrimination separately scored |
| Valid measured temporal difference in a matched task | Highlight the actual interval and model the phrase chunk | Temporal observation, not global fluency grade |
| Supported targeted correction | Brief actionable articulation/rhythm cue, A/B replay, one retry | Dimension-specific feedback, supporting evidence IDs and repair outcome |
| Message recovered with no reliable target issue | Acknowledge success and ask a new meaningful question | Successful lexical recovery; pronunciation remains unassessed where appropriate |

For a zero-Russian learner, self-repair means an attainable choice or imitation after a model, not “spot every error.” Introduce IPA only on demand. Ordinary speed is the reference; slower playback can aid inspection but does not define the desired timing. Avoid chaining a new sound, unfamiliar script, new vocabulary and a new case ending in the same diagnostic item.

### Feature pipeline after the first useful release

Add a real speech-activity detector as a separately versioned estimate, retain margins and original quiet consonants, and check difficult onsets/offsets. Use Russian MFA or another evaluated phone aligner only after lexical matching; store pronunciation variants and dictionary version. An aligned expected phoneme is an imposed hypothesis, not an observed phone identity. Detect omissions/misalignment rather than assigning every expected phone to some acoustic interval.

For stressed-vowel duration and vowel quality, derive F1/F2 tracks with confidence/track-stability checks and context labels; compare within speaker and comparable neighboring consonants. A formant tracker may lock onto harmonics, especially with higher F0 or poor recordings. For palatalization, examine transition trajectories and releases in defined consonant/vowel contexts, not a broad “softness” axis. For intonation, use normalized F0 contours over aligned voiced regions with octave-error handling and phrase-boundary labels. Preserve unvoiced gaps; do not interpolate a fluent contour through them. For fluency, combine pause locations, speech/articulation rates and actual repairs with the task type. None of these future features is implemented by the current STFT/pitch display.

An initial feature can remain a visualization and a tentative contrast suggestion while its diagnostic thresholds are evaluated. Do not fabricate a numerical error bound from a model card. Distinguish timestamp grid resolution, observed boundary error, periodicity, repeated-run variability and calibrated error probability.

## Evaluation that improves the teacher without burdening the friend

The machine-readable [fixture set](fixtures/speech_teacher_v1.json) separates public TTS prompts from evidence-policy cases. All acoustic fixtures start `not_recorded`; intended text is not a gold transcript of nonexistent audio. These cases can drive adapter checks before a labeled speech corpus exists.

The speech-provider lane subsequently generated an actual 13.36-second public Qwen sample containing greeting, lock/castle stress, `всё`, a question and a mathematical sentence. Its [audit record](fixtures/qwen_public_russian_v1.audit.json) records the verified WAV shape, exact byte hash, source text and model provenance. This lane independently checked the container/hash binding, then ran pinned local BF16 ASR without the source text. It recovered most words but rendered intended `замо́к` as `самоек`, `за́мок` as `заямок`, and `Всё` as `Все`. This is a **suspected target issue**, not a proven phonetic defect: TTS generation, ASR decoding, stress marks and ё normalization remain alternative explanations. Do not treat this sample as a verified stress reference.

Root then authorized one [controlled unaccented comparison](fixtures/qwen_public_russian_unaccented_v1.audit.json). The TTS lane removed only U+0301 marks, preserving text/context, `Всё`, punctuation, voice, seed and model settings. Independent ASR recovered both `замок` tokens and all other words apart from the remaining `Всё/Все` spelling ambiguity. The new 15.84-second sample decoded in 7.783 seconds with peak RSS 4,363,059,200 bytes and peak Metal allocation 5,258,106,284 bytes. This supports a plain-text synthesis frontend candidate for this backend; it does **not** reveal which syllable carried stress. Keep pedagogically stressed display text separate from exact synthesis text and record both with provenance. A later explicit-stress backend comparator can test the difficult homographs; no additional model download is needed to finish this controlled result. No human labeling gate is introduced.

The successful ASR operation took 11.737 seconds including model verification/loading, with peak RSS 4,355,702,784 bytes and peak Metal allocation 5,191,502,392 bytes. The one-shot process exited and released its model. Thirteen CPU-only boundary/policy tests passed. These are one public-sample measurements, not a novice-Russian benchmark. No aligner was downloaded or run; the worker explicitly returns null word timestamps, confidence and acoustic assessment, retaining exact original-frame crop and processing timestamps instead.

**TTS sample audit:** generate every item with exact text, stress marks preserved, fixed provider/model/voice/settings, and content hash; ordinary and explicitly careful delivery are separate conditions. Record whether combining acute marks are ignored, pronounced as symbols, or alter stress. Blind lexical ASR screens skipped/added/repeated words; it does not verify homograph stress or naturalness. Independently listen to full phrases and target crops. For homographs compare both senses in context; for soft/hard contrasts listen to consonant transitions/releases; for `все/всё` check lexical difference. Store each result as `pass / suspected_issue / unresolved`, listener identity/capability and evidence span, never “native verified” merely because TTS generated it. A sample with a suspected target defect should be replaced or regenerated before teaching that contrast, while usable samples remain available. The optional friend's reaction is welcome but not required.

**First local benchmark:** compare the pinned Qwen BF16 pipeline and Parakeet on the same public/licensed Russian samples and the fixture set; later include Ember's voluntarily recorded attempts. Keep native read speech, generated speech, novice imitation and spontaneous speech as separate slices. Report token omissions/substitutions, inability to recover a message, invalid timestamps, time to feedback and peak memory. A TTS-only score cannot estimate novice performance. Retain raw and normalized transcripts; preserve `е/ё` distinctions and stress-sensitive targets outside generic WER normalization.

**Assess feedback, not only transcription:** count false corrections, actionable repairs, evidence-free claims, correct uncertainty handling, and next-attempt/novel-phrase outcomes. With small samples report counts and examples, not a precise generalization percentage. Agreement with another model is not ground truth. Optional expert audits of a small set can later measure diagnosis precision; neither that work nor a complete corpus should block demonstrations, perception practice, tentative listening suggestions, or normal learning.

**Adversarial checks:** empty/silent/noisy audio; wrong target phrase; changed homograph sense with identical audio; two recordings with identical transcript but different realization; stretched pauses; octave-shifted pitch tracking; clipped consonant onset; stale attempt hash; out-of-range timestamp; listening hypothesis that mentions a sound absent from the crop; text-only provider claiming to have heard a sound. Policy fixtures specify the required action and prohibited claim. Any confident acoustic diagnosis from absent audio is a contract failure. The teacher should still return a useful demonstration or retry action.

## Implementation seams and order

The subsequent [controlled Silero acoustic audit](SILERO_STRESS_AUDIT.md) supplies an actual two-recording spectral/timing example and reproducible replay artifacts. It supports a region-comparison instrument: longer candidate vowel regions switch syllables, while pitch/level peaks do not. It also demonstrates why naïve LPC pole ordering cannot yet supply dependable formant feedback.

1. **Implemented:** local pinned ASR worker, bounded gateway operation and recording transport, with serial inference, cancellation/deadlines and no runtime model download.
2. **Implemented:** separate exact-recording transcription and learner confirmation facts in core/sync v3; language feedback binds the confirmation. Original observations remain immutable and retain their provenance.
3. **Accepted in the app journey:** recorded audio → independent transcript → confirm/edit → Claude language feedback. Continue improving replay/contrast/try-again controls while preserving the previous attempt. Transcript disagreement remains editable meaning confirmation, not a pronunciation grade.
4. Add the optional Gemini listening adapter with explicit selected-audio disclosure and no automatic recording upload. Expose source/model and whether the suggestion came from measurements or an audio listener. Its failure should leave local practice usable.
5. **Public audit completed:** Qwen accented/unaccented controls and Silero explicit-stress acoustic comparison have exact sample hashes, results and resource measurements. Extend the acoustic pipeline one target at a time, especially the region-comparison instrument demonstrated here. Maintain app installation work independently.

No core/schema/provider source was modified by this research lane; root integrated those paths. The local worker and pinned setup live in `scripts/speech/asr_*` and `setup-asr.py`, with their own cache/environment under `.local/qwen3-asr`. The pure post-recognition `asr_review.plan_next_action` offers bounded reference-replacement requests and learner listen/confirm/retry actions; any caller dispatching replacements must persist its count. It preserves ё/е as a visible ambiguity without automatically regenerating for that spelling difference alone. The recording-to-confirmed-language-feedback loop now works. Precise word localization, stable vowel-region instrumentation, audio-listener hypotheses and calibrated phonetic diagnosis remain the next additions.
