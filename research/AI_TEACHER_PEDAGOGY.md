# An AI teacher that develops usable Russian

Research and implementation audit, 2026-11-05. This extends [the evidence review](EVIDENCE.md), [efficient learning models](OPTIMAL_LEARNING.md), and [the curriculum extension](CURRICULUM_NEXT.md). The recommendation is a structured, adaptive teacher: meaningful input, concise explicit explanation, comprehension that requires the target distinction, supported production, corrective feedback, and later independent use. Neither chat alone nor spaced flashcards cover that loop.

Ember begins with no Russian, wants all four skills, and wants intellectually interesting material. The teacher supplies explanations and speech examples. Casual conversations with a friend remain optional real uses of Russian; the friend has no curriculum, recording, labeling, or assessment obligations. Variable working-memory demands call for adjustable support, not a fixed diagnosis or a cap on conceptual ambition.

## Evidence and what it does—and does not—license

The rows distinguish direct intervention evidence, syntheses, and our transfer hypotheses. Most evidence concerns other language pairs. A good component is not proof that the combined Rufren policy is optimal. Source IDs E/O refer to the linked existing reviews. Newly acquired full text and an acquisition manifest are in `~/paperbin/rufren/ai-teacher-pedagogy/`; earlier full text is in the parent directory. Abstract-only sources are identified rather than silently treated as full-text reviews.

| ID / source | Result and evidential boundary | Implementable consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 — [Spada & Tomita (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00562.x), 41-study meta-analysis; publisher abstract read | Explicit instruction had larger effects than implicit instruction for both simple and complex English grammatical features; benefits included controlled knowledge and spontaneous use. This does not establish an ideal explanation length, a universal grammar order, or that explaining alone produces fluent speech. | Offer direct, compact explanations even for sophisticated grammar. Pair each explanation with understanding and use; measure spontaneous use separately from explaining the rule. |
| P2 — [Shintani (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/amu067), 42 experiments in 33 studies; indexed publisher abstract | Processing instruction outperformed production-based instruction for receptive knowledge. Production-based instruction was comparable overall for productive knowledge and superior when both groups received the same explicit information. Cross-study comparisons do not prescribe one universal ordering. | Include meaning-bearing comprehension **and** production. Do not treat success in interpreting an ending as evidence of retrieving it in speech. Russian-specific E9 provides a narrower complementary example. |
| P3 / E2 — [Karpicke & Roediger (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408); author-hosted full text read | With 40 Swahili–English word pairs, continuing retrieval after an initial correct answer benefited one-week recall much more than continuing study alone. This is word-pair recall, not a trial of conversational fluency. | Revisit successful items; hide the answer during retrieval. Provide teaching when retrieval is unavailable, rather than repeatedly demanding an unknown answer. |
| E1 — [Kim & Webb (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479), 48 experiments; abstract | Spacing benefits delayed L2 learning; equal and expanding schedules were statistically equivalent in this synthesis. | Use the transparent spacing baseline. Learn a personal memory model only when data can distinguish its predictions; no evidence here validates the current exact interval ladder. |
| O4 — [Nakata & Suzuki (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12581), 115 learners; existing full-text copy | Interleaved English grammar practice produced more errors during training but better one-week grammaticality judgments than blocked practice. Its outcome is not unrestricted speaking. | After introducing a contrast, mix confusable alternatives so selecting the construction is part of the task. Keep task mechanics stable. Immediate accuracy is not the sole policy objective. |
| E5 — [Lyster & Saito (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263109990520), 15 studies / 827 learners; existing full text | Classroom oral feedback benefited learning; prompts exceeded recasts in the synthesis. This does not establish AI judgment reliability or that every error should interrupt a conversation. | Give a specific repair opportunity when the learner plausibly knows the form; otherwise demonstrate and explain. Offer one high-value correction and return to meaning. |
| E3 / E4 — [Uchihara et al. perception synthesis (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263125100879), [production transfer synthesis (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000195); existing full text | Varied-speaker perceptual practice helps perception. Transfer to production is smaller and evidence for durability/generalization is limited. | Use listening identification and actual production as related but distinct targets. Vary speakers/contexts gradually; no claim that a particular number of voices is optimal. |
| P4 — [Gass, Winke, Isbell & Ahn (2019)](https://doi.org/10.64152/10125/44684); full text, two eye-tracking studies | Caption use and its relation to comprehension/working memory differed between Spanish and English learner groups. Small samples, exploratory subgrouping, and no independent proficiency premeasure limit causal claims. | Make captions, replay, and chunks available and record their use. Do not infer that all low-memory learners require the same presentation, or ban text in teaching. Separately test audio-only understanding. |
| P5 — [Nakanishi (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.157), 34 studies / 3,942 learners; publisher abstract | Extensive reading improved reading outcomes; group-comparison effect d = .46, versus .71 for pre/post contrasts. This does not establish that difficult authentic text plus many glosses equals extensive reading. | Keep an accessible, enjoyable reading strand as well as close work on desired difficult passages. Repeated decoding and comprehension need volume, not only explanatory depth. |
| P6 — [Cheon et al. (2025)](https://hdl.handle.net/10125/109443), four-week randomized field experiment; full text | 363 assigned, 248 retained after outlier/dropout exclusions, with greater loss in AI groups. Unrestricted AI improved lexical-diversity results relative to scheduled human conversation; matched-schedule AI improvement was not significant. Outcome was transcript-derived lexical diversity, not a comprehensive speaking assessment. | AI conversation is credible practice infrastructure, but this trial does not prove general superiority or beginner Russian effectiveness. Track continued use and independent performance, not only gains among completers. |
| P7 — [Kestin et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6), college physics RCT; full text | Structured AI tutoring improved immediate posttests against active classroom instruction in two lessons. The authors report that prompting alone did not reliably scaffold multipart problems, so the application enforced sequence and supplied detailed solution content. This is neither a language trial nor delayed-retention evidence. | Give the teacher explicit task state, target material and reference answers. Use the model for responsive explanation and variation within a reliable lesson flow. Do not infer efficacy for an unrestricted chat interface. |

These findings support combining instruction, practice and feedback. They do not establish a special retrieval percentage, a mandated daily dose, or one fastest path through Russian. Interest, effort, time and eventual purposes belong in the objective, not as nuisances to eliminate.

## The teaching policy

### Teach a useful contrast, then use it

An initial construction episode should have an executable contract rather than only a block of explanatory prose:

1. **Encounter a message.** Supply one short exchange or sentence with a communicative purpose, natural-speed audio, ordinary spelling, and accessible meaning. Store the exact displayed/heard stimulus. Listening before text can be offered; do not turn that ordering into a universal rule.
2. **Explain the distinction.** Describe what changes in meaning and show one diagnostic contrast. Keep a compact default; allow a deeper explanation without losing the learner's draft. For case, deliberately separate participant role from word order, using appropriate discourse context.
3. **Interpret.** Ask a meaning question whose answer genuinely depends on that distinction. If both a correct rule and a plausible wrong rule predict the same response, the item can teach but cannot distinguish those rules.
4. **Produce with adjustable support.** Ask for a small meaningful answer; allow a frame or cue. Record which support was visible before the response. Sentence imitation, slot substitution, independent formulation and extended conversation are different tasks.
5. **Repair one useful thing.** Acknowledge the intended meaning, identify the consequential mismatch, and give a short cue or model. Let the learner retry or continue. An immediate repair is valuable practice, not independent evidence of durable acquisition.
6. **Return to meaning and revisit.** Use the feature in a changed message, then revisit it after a delay. Preserve an independent first attempt before answer revelation. Scheduled review can use practiced material; generalization assessment needs genuinely new material.

This sequence is a reasonable initial policy, not a result proved by a single experiment. It should be interruptible: “show me,” “smaller step,” “why?”, replay, skip, and learner-initiated conversation all remain valid actions. A zero beginner needs an understandable model before repeated output demands; a learner already able to use the form can skip redundant explanation.

### Feedback should diagnose the task, not the person

The teacher must receive the exact prompt, response, assistance history, target dimension, and acceptable variation. The current `AnswerFeedbackRequest` already binds feedback to actual task identities and distinguishes written text from transcripts; retain that. A reference is an example, not necessarily the only correct wording. Correct Russian word-order, aspect or register alternatives may change nuance without being errors.

Use error categories that inform an action: message misunderstood, wrong participant mapping, missing lexical item, inflection selection, spelling/keyboard issue, or insufficient acoustic evidence. An unfamiliar word can cause a grammar-task error; a bad transcript can cause a false language correction. An uncertain judgment should trigger a smaller explanation, contrast, replay or clarifying attempt, not a forced diagnosis. Acoustic feedback requires audio; textual feedback can still be useful while pronunciation assessment is unavailable.

The “one correction” default is an engineering choice intended to keep the exchange usable, not a quantified law. Select a correction that blocks the message or matches the current target. Keep an optional detailed correction view. During open conversation, a short post-turn correction can preserve the conversational goal; focused practice can interrupt sooner. Ask for self-repair only when the required knowledge is plausible—otherwise the prompt becomes an unhelpful guessing exercise.

### Working-memory support without impoverishing the content

Keep the current task, interlocutors and intended message visible. Separate a demanding new sound, a new script mapping, and a new inflection when they obscure one another. Offer phrase-sized replay, slower playback alongside ordinary speed, persistent vocabulary glosses, and partial response frames. A sophisticated explanation of quantifier scope can coexist with producing a three-word Russian answer.

Fade one support within a skill when independent performance warrants trying it; restore it on request without treating restoration as failure. Caption-supported comprehension and audio-only comprehension are both useful, different observations. Do not interpret device latency, long deliberation, or a single rough session as a trait measure. No source reviewed here validates a fixed working-memory score as Rufren's curriculum controller.

### Interesting reading as both destination and practice

Use two complementary reading paths. One supplies short, comprehensible recurring stories and conversations, including science-fiction settings, where reading for meaning is feasible without inspecting every word. The other approaches a personally wanted literary or technical passage with glosses, a construction explanation and optional simplification. Keep the original and generated adaptation distinct.

Select prerequisite teaching by *marginal access to a wanted meaning*: a recurring construction, useful word sense, inflection, or discourse connective may unlock more than another isolated noun. Maintain ordinary conversational usefulness too. This passage-based planning objective is our hypothesis, not an established optimal algorithm. Gloss count and token coverage cannot by themselves measure understanding of irony, a proof, or a philosophical argument.

Include a meaning response: choose a continuation, explain who did what, ask the character a question, or summarize a small claim. Later write a new sentence using the useful structure. Avoid making every enjoyable passage an exam or converting every encountered token into a review obligation. Successful real conversation can motivate the next lesson without requiring any recording or grading by the friend.

## Audit of the implemented core

This is an audit of the source inspected at the start of this research task, before parallel implementation changes. It identifies specific seams, not a claim that those issues remain after integration.

| Observed implementation | Consequence | Required improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ContrastPolicy` uses fixed response likelihoods and heuristic action scores | A transparent heuristic is useful, but it is not POMDP planning or an optimized curriculum | Name it accurately; keep it as a comparison policy. Add a real action-transition model before claiming lookahead optimization. |
| `last_learning` derives from recorded teaching decisions | Recommending or opening an action can masquerade as actual instruction; repeated selection can postpone a probe | Separate selected, presented, attempted and completed. Start exposure/revisit timing from observed presentation or practice, not intent. Aborted actions must not count as learning. |
| `GrammarBeliefs::observe` treats response classes as evidence of a stable latent rule | A learner changes after instruction; repeated same-item answers are dependent; correct subject-first answers may be nondiagnostic | Condition evidence on the stimulus's distinguishing predictions. Track item/context repetition and teaching transitions; allow uncertainty and learning between attempts. |
| One fixed `novel_probe_revision`, with a `novel_context` boolean | A probe stops being unseen once exposed. A new revision may only change punctuation or repeat the answer in another screen | Track rendered stimulus identity/family and actual exposure, including answer previews. Select an unused probe for novelty; reused probes remain valid retention practice. |
| `AskCoach` and `coach_reviewed` content gates | Experimental learning can depend on unavailable human labor, contrary to the product's teaching model | Replace the ordinary fallback with AI explanation/demonstration or another task. Separate content origin/quality flags from human signoff. Human judgments remain optional evidence sources. |
| `FeatureEvidence` attaches whole-attempt judgments to every tagged target | A correct overall answer can overcredit every feature; an incorrect answer can implicate unrelated features | Distinguish targets exposed, targets necessary to solve the item, and targets directly judged. Without direct linkage, describe observations rather than feature mastery. |
| `SpacedReview` uses exact revision × broad skill and self-report/recall judgments | Meaning recall, spelling, imitation and grammatical production can share a broad channel but have different evidence; content edits reset exact-revision history | Preserve the simple baseline, but give a richer planner task/dimension/cue-aware views. Link equivalent stimulus families without automatically transferring correctness. |
| Hand-set forecast and constant comparator probabilities | A numerical difference can look like measured learning advantage | Treat these as synthetic forecasts. Never display them as estimated personal gains. Prefer a prediction only where observed outcomes can assess calibration. |
| Fixed 70% judgment-confidence filtering | A source's self-reported confidence is not empirically calibrated correctness | Keep source, dimension and uncertainty distinct. Avoid treating the threshold as a cross-provider reliability guarantee. |

Useful existing boundaries should survive: immutable events, separate skills, cue recording, durable decisions, competing judgments, future-event filtering, and prompt-bound AI feedback. Improve their semantics rather than replacing the event history with an opaque “mastery” number.

## A genuine next model beyond heuristic scores

Start with a *small, time-varying model for one construction*, not a whole-language simulated learner. Represent several separable causes: prerequisite word meaning unavailable, target cue not noticed, competing interpretation preferred, form understood but not retrievable in the required output, and unmodeled difficulty. The states are engineering hypotheses; a response may fit more than one.

For each authored item, record which hypotheses predict which responses. For each teaching action, specify a modest transition hypothesis and a time/effort range. Enumerate short sequences—explain then interpret; contrast then produce; retrieve then repair—and evaluate expected delayed task performance minus time/effort cost. Include parameter uncertainty. If the preferred action changes under small plausible changes to an invented parameter, choose a robust simple action or let the learner choose instead of advertising false precision.

This is the implementable connection to [Rafferty et al.'s POMDP work](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12290). It differs materially from maximizing information gain or adding a “Bayesian” label to scores. Diagnostics are useful when they change what the teacher should do. Demonstrations and explanation can be valuable even when they provide little immediate diagnostic information.

For memory, first collect interpretable task-specific retrieval histories and compare forecasts against the fixed ladder. [Pavlik & Anderson](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.14.2.101) motivates explicitly valuing retention gain per practice time; it does not identify Ember's parameters. With sparse data, share conservative priors across similar tasks while retaining separate evidence. Do not fit dozens of latent factors from a few sessions or use generic model confidence as the learner's recall probability.

The content model should propose and explain candidate actions; core code should enforce availability, task boundaries, evidence capture and scheduling. High-quality local/API inference is valuable for Russian quality checks, alternatives, explanations and interesting content. It cannot repair missing outcome definitions or turn an invented transition table into empirical learning science.

## Evaluation that helps everyday learning

Routine sessions should work immediately, with cached teaching and ordinary correction. Evaluation is lightweight product improvement, not permission to learn.

| Question | Small useful check | What would change the policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did teaching improve the intended construction? | Occasional delayed first attempts on unused items, separately in comprehension and production; retain assistance/time/effort | Repeated trained-item success without new-item use calls for contrasting examples and variable production, not a larger mastery badge |
| Are automatic corrections useful? | Inspect a sample of accepted alternatives, intentionally ambiguous answers and known errors; record false corrections and learner disagreements | Frequent false corrections in a domain lower automatic authority and trigger better references or narrower feedback |
| Do extra scaffolds help? | Compare supported practice followed by an independent check; monitor effort and voluntary replay | Keep scaffolds that improve later independent use or make practice sustainable; do not remove them merely to increase difficulty |
| Is the model better than the baseline? | Assign matched new content to alternative policies when feasible, with equal-ish practice opportunity and delayed probes | Prefer simpler policy when benefits are absent/fragile; include uncertainty and cross-item transfer rather than claiming causal certainty from a streak |
| Is speech training transferring? | Novel words/sentences and voices; separate perception, acoustic feature measurements, intelligibility and fluency | Better identification alone does not complete production training; transcript scores never establish accent quality |
| Is wanted reading becoming accessible? | A short previously unseen passage plus a meaning response, gloss use and optional effort | Adjust content/prerequisite selection if familiar-text fluency does not transfer |

Useful implementation checks are narrower than a full experiment: an unopened decision must not advance learning; a shown answer must make that attempt assisted; a reused probe must cease being unseen; an ambiguous case item must not strongly discriminate rule hypotheses; valid alternative wording must survive feedback; no request to a human may be required for the teacher's next normal step.

An evaluation fallback should be “useful practice, uncertain inference.” It should never be “no Russian until a friend has labeled it.”
