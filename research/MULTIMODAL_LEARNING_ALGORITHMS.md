# Adaptive selection for multimodal Russian practice

Research/design recommendation, 2026-11-06. This extends [optimal learning](OPTIMAL_LEARNING.md) and [personal review policy](PERSONAL_REVIEW_POLICY.md), rather than replacing protocol-8 timing. No new scheduler or learner model is implemented by this note.

**Recommendation:** keep an inspectable task-graph baseline for choosing the next learning action, with bounded exact-task spacing for returns. Evaluate a small condition-aware Bayesian state model as a challenger. Use default-parameter FSRS as a *forecast comparator on compatible exact recall tasks*, not as the curriculum engine for listening, handwriting, copying, speech and grammar. Current evidence supports testing adaptation; it does not identify a universally optimal Russian sequence or justify fitting a large model to one person's sparse history.

## What the algorithms solve

| Approach | Useful contribution | Evidence and limits for Rufren |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FSRS-7 | Predict next exact-card recall from timed review/grade history; translate a selected retention target into intervals. | Strong large-scale chronological flashcard forecast benchmark. It does not choose explanatory actions, infer handwriting quality, or establish cross-modal transfer. Benchmark rank is not randomized retention gained per minute. |
| HLR | Compact exponential-forgetting prediction with learner/item/history features. | Duolingo's original model pools a substantial language-learning population. Its observation channel and feature assumptions do not turn a sentence grade into successful recall of every component word. See the existing policy note for dataset and intervention details. |
| DASH | Pool item difficulty, learner ability and timed study history to personalize review. | Has a classroom learning intervention, not just a prediction result. Population pooling is central to its response to sparse individual item history; a single Rufren learner does not supply that population. |
| BKT / BKT+forget / intervention-aware variants | Separate hidden learning state, noisy observed response and transitions after instruction. Cue-dependent emissions and action-dependent transitions are conceptually useful. | Requires a defensible skill/task decomposition. Binary latent state is a model assumption, not observed mastery. Fitting independent guess/slip/learn/forget parameters to each sparse modality would be poorly identified. |
| Deep knowledge tracing, attention and transformer variants | Learn complex interaction-sequence predictors on multi-learner datasets. | Candidate research comparators if suitable data exist, not a credible first fit on Ember's personal log. Predictive success still does not identify which teaching action causes the most learning. |
| ACT-R / MEMORIZE / SELECT / POMDP control | Express an objective and choose timing/actions under explicit learning and cost assumptions. | Existing notes cover these papers. Their optimality is conditional on a learner model, action set, horizon and cost. A bounded voluntary mixed session changes the optimization problem. |

The current [official SRS benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark/tree/4ddb9fd9d6d7888ea52fc721d5ab0e6dd7a5e877) evaluates chronological flashcard forecasts. Its FSRS-7 family is competitive; default parameters are a relevant cold-start comparator. Importantly, its DASH implementation omits item Difficulty because that component is not applicable to the dataset. A DASH ranking in that benchmark therefore does not directly test the original classroom system. The README also distinguishes prediction-optimized FSRS from scheduling penalties that trade some predictive fit for more reasonable proposed intervals. These are different objectives, even within one algorithm family. [Official DASH code](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark/blob/4ddb9fd9d6d7888ea52fc721d5ab0e6dd7a5e877/models/dash.py).

## Evidence that changes the decision

**Personalization can improve learning, but appropriate baselines matter.** Lindsey et al. studied 179 eighth-grade Spanish students using typed English-to-Spanish vocabulary/short-sentence translation. Items were randomly assigned within students to massed, generic spaced or personalized review. Cumulative tests occurred at semester end and a month later. At the second test the authors report a 10.0% retention improvement over generic spacing and 16.5% over massing; these are the paper’s reported comparison figures, not a forecast of Rufren’s benefit. Its DASH model explicitly borrows information across learners and items; it is not evidence that two observations identify an individual item's forgetting dynamics. [Author-hosted full paper](https://home.cs.colorado.edu/~mozer/Research/Selected%20Publications/reprints/LindseyShroyerPashlerMozer2014Published.pdf).

Mettler, Massey and Kellman compared ARTS adaptive sequencing against fixed schedules, including schedules yoked to adaptive participants. Their geography-name/location task had repeated multiple-choice responses and a one-week delayed test. Adaptive sequencing performed better, including against yoked conditions. This helps separate personalization from merely choosing a better average spacing pattern. It does **not** license treating elapsed time while an iPad app is open as retrieval fluency; ARTS relied on response times in its controlled interaction. [Open author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6028005/).

**A direction is not interchangeable with its reverse.** Bernardi et al. (2024) tested productive and receptive translation practice across three experiments. Participants retrieved translations three times correctly in one direction and then practiced the reverse direction. Transfer occurred in both orders but was partial: learning one direction did not remove the need to practice the other. This supports separate directional opportunities, not zero transfer and not automatic promotion of all sibling tasks. Access here is the primary article's abstract, not its full methods/results. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2024.2397043); [abstract record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39222444).

**Cues change both what a response measures and what practice teaches.** Zhang et al. (2026) analyze 688 middle-school students randomly assigned to nine arithmetic cue conditions. Their cue-conditioned BKT separates guess/slip from learn/forget parameters; immediate support and later learning need not move together. The authors explicitly caution that roughly 70–80 students per condition and moderate predictive performance do not yet support deploying their fitted model as a personalized tutor. This is useful evidence against pooling assisted and unassisted correctness. It is not evidence to give Russian learners misleading cues: the manipulation involved arithmetic spacing/color, and the authors report discrepancies between some model-derived and behavioral classifications. [Open full paper](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1520114.pdf).

**Fading needs a learning objective, not a cosmetic disappearance of help.** Atkinson, Renkl and Merrill (2003) report that fading worked solution steps combined with principle-focused self-explanation improved near and far transfer in two experiments; fading alone had less reliable far-transfer effects. This is an analogy for grammar construction, not a Russian vocabulary sequencing result. Access here is the university's article abstract. [Primary university record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/transitioning-from-studying-examples-to-solving-problems-effects-/).

**Current KT tooling does not solve the data problem.** The [pyBKT paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.00385v2) supplies reproducible BKT variants, parameter-fitting and prediction checks; its data-sufficiency experiments use simulated canonical models. Its reported sample recommendations are not a minimum-data guarantee for Rufren's heterogeneous tasks. The [official library](https://github.com/CAHLR/pyBKT/tree/0f4ea412e18f6bcbd396fdf2be68fdd43aebebb6) supports multiple emission/learning classes and forgetting. The [pyKT benchmark paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/75ca2b23d9794f02a92449af65a57556-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf) documents evaluation inconsistencies, including label leakage when one multi-component question is expanded into consecutive labeled interactions. Rufren must likewise not count one assisted sentence answer as several independent component outcomes. The [current official pyKT inventory](https://github.com/pykt-team/pykt-toolkit) includes SAKT, AKT, simpleKT, FoLiBiKT and DTransformer: there is a substantial modern comparison suite, rather than one universally established winner to import. Their presence in a toolkit is not itself evidence of a learning intervention or suitability for a single cold-start learner.

## Implementable baseline: directional task graph, bounded selection

This is our engineering recommendation, informed by the evidence above; the exact policy and thresholds are testable product choices.

Keep one family for a word/sense or construction, with separate authored task nodes:

- **Hear → meaning:** a sound recording is required task input. Replaying, slowing or adding a transcript are separate assistance actions. A new speaker/recording supplies a transfer probe, not another success on the old acoustic token.
- **Read → meaning:** the Russian spelling is task input. Translation or a meaningful illustration that discloses the answer is assistance. Success does not establish listening or Russian production.
- **Sound → handwrite:** score orthographic content separately from motor legibility and recognizer uncertainty. ASR/OCR/transcription approval is not a direct measurement of the whole skill. Recognition should be able to abstain and request comparison.
- **Visible word → copy → say:** useful guided practice, with the written answer present. Copying is exposure and motor rehearsal, not unaided spelling. Reading a visible word aloud is distinct from producing it from meaning, and transcript correctness is distinct from pronunciation.
- **Construction → new message:** later add controlled slot variation and confusable alternatives. A changed UUID or noun alone does not guarantee meaningful transfer; preserve which features/context genuinely changed.

At each voluntary session, construct a small eligible set: repair a currently troublesome task, return to an actually due exact task, offer an underpracticed direction whose prerequisites have been encountered, or introduce fresh meaningful content. Respect the learner's time/skill choice and pause/snooze settings. Keep the session bounded and reserve room for new language. Use explicit priority tiers, then oldest eligible opportunity and stable tie breaking, instead of an opaque score with many arbitrary weights.

Within a task, use a declared support ladder: complete model → partial completion/constraint → independent response → independent delayed return → changed-context probe. A successful assisted answer permits teaching to continue; it does not satisfy the independent rung. Following a clean error, save the response first, give useful feedback, then offer a supported repair. Offer a later fresh independent opportunity before making a delayed-success claim. Help remains available, and the learner can choose easier practice without the app interpreting that choice as inability.

Do not force this ladder into a rigid requirement to finish every modality for every word. Choose relevant directions, allow uneven development, and show a concrete next action. For grammar, brief explanation or comparison of contrasting examples can matter more than another repetition of the anchor sentence. Difficulty should be changed locally—such as distractor similarity or amount of completion support—without making an unvalidated universal “85% correct” target the whole curriculum.

## Challenger: a small condition-aware Bayesian state model

The next challenger should test *one narrow family of actions*, not fit a universal language state. A practical initial scope is choosing independent recall versus a partial cue versus a complete model for a small set of spelling/meaning tasks.

Use a two-state Bayesian learning hypothesis per family × direction, with elapsed-time forgetting. Keep separate observation models for independent exact criterion responses and supported responses; missing/contested/recognizer-uncertain outcomes produce no binary likelihood. Apply a declared learning transition **after** practice/feedback. An assisted response can be a learning opportunity without becoming a clean success likelihood. The latent state must stay an internal hypothesis, not a displayed mastery percentage.

Cold start with broad, shared, fixed priors. Do not estimate learner ability, item difficulty, modality emissions, forgetting, action gains and cross-modal transfer simultaneously from one short history. Initially fix those assumptions and compare sensitivity settings. Default to zero credited transfer between directions, with a small plausible-transfer setting as sensitivity analysis; do not claim the transfer coefficients were learned. Pool only genuinely comparable authored tasks, and retain an unmodeled-difficulty/abstain route.

Use the model first in shadow. For action selection, forecast alone is insufficient: an easy supported task may predict high correctness while teaching little. A short-horizon challenger must also specify expected learning transition, time/effort cost and terminal independent performance. Those transitions cannot be causally identified from a passive log in which the tutor chooses help exactly when the learner struggles. A small prospective randomized action comparison is needed before fitting action gains.

FSRS with pinned defaults remains an additional exact-recall forecast comparator. Keep its supported input mapping narrow; do not fabricate Hard/Easy from latency or prose, and do not treat all modalities as one card. A small half-life state model remains useful for the spacing-only shadow forecast described in the prior note. Neither comparator chooses the curriculum graph by itself.

## Prospective evaluation that can reject the challenger

1. **Verify observation capture first.** Commit a first response or explicit inability before revealing answer-bearing support; record task input versus help, exact action version, modality, actual start, support chronology and repair ancestry. Preserve original decisions when late evidence requalifies an outcome. Current protocol-8 timing snapshots are useful audit records but lack full randomized selection/forecast contracts.
2. **Forecast test.** Freeze predictions before new independent starts. Compare constant/recency, the bounded-history baseline forecast, compatible FSRS defaults and the challenger. Report log loss/Brier score, calibration, covered/excluded outcomes and actual delay distributions by direction. Hold out the future chronologically and keep sibling questions/expanded components together. Assisted correctness cannot leak into the independent target.
3. **Action test at equal offered budget.** Randomize matched task families to the explicit graph baseline or challenger, keeping content, feedback, help availability and practice budget comparable. Assign all sibling modalities of a family together to reduce interference. Record candidate sets, policy/parameter versions, assignment and action actually issued. A later within-family crossover has carryover; it is not a clean reset.
4. **Common delayed endpoint.** Preselect a horizon from introduction, such as two weeks, independently of each scheduler's last review. Test both practiced exact tasks and truly withheld speakers/words/combinations. Score spelling, meaning and speech evidence separately. Record first response, then teach; probes should not become a large extra burden.
5. **Judge usefulness.** Compare delayed independent outcomes per allocated practice minute, assistance demand, completion burden and learner preference. Report actual learning time separately from network or abandoned-tab time. Early forecast gains without learning gains are insufficient. Sparse personal data may support a practical preference or expose a bad assumption without delivering a precise causal effect estimate.

Reject or simplify a challenger that loses to recency, depends heavily on unverifiable transfer assumptions, consumes more time/help for the same delayed outcome, or improves practiced answers without useful changed-context performance. No claim of “optimal” follows from merely fitting historical responses better.
