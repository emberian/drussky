# Sound Focus: hearing meaning, then saying it

Pedagogical design and source audit, 2026-11-06. Sound Focus should be a short part of learning usable Russian, not a requirement to perfect isolated sounds before conversation. The AI teacher provides models, explanations and correction; no friend is assigned recording, labeling or review duties. This note specifies a teaching design, not an acoustically validated product or an optimal training dose.

## A small complete learning loop

Use one contrast at a time: **лук / люк** (onion / hatch here) or **за́мок / замо́к** (castle / lock). The meanings are useful anchors; a hatch can soon enter a small science-fiction scene. Teach a tiny scene separately rather than making a novice solve unfamiliar vocabulary, grammar and sound distinctions in the same blind trial.

1. **Labelled teaching.** Show both words and their meanings. Let the learner play each model and compare them. Give one short explanation and an optional articulation or stress detail. Establish both meaning mappings before testing them. Keep controls and labels visible so the task does not require remembering a vanished instruction.
2. **Four blind identification trials.** Two examples of each member, shuffled without strict alternation. This is a product starting dose; offer another short round or stop. Present two familiar meaning choices and play one member. “Blind” means the playing member is not disclosed before the response, not that both meaning labels disappear. No timer is needed. Keep choice positions stable within a short round; order can be counterbalanced between rounds and must be recorded. Neither a fixed response side nor filenames, captions, icons or highlighting should disclose the selected sound.
3. **Specific feedback.** Commit the first choice, then show the intended meaning and word, replay it, and optionally compare the other member. One sentence is enough: “That was люк, a hatch. Compare лук, an onion.” An immediate retry is supported practice, not a new independent success. A fresh mixed trial lets the learner use the distinction again. Use an explicit Next control so feedback stays available as long as needed.
4. **Chosen-meaning production.** Ask which meaning the learner wants to say, then record a fresh take. Offer the model on demand and retain whether it was heard before speaking. A model-then-imitation take is useful practice; a meaning-cued take before the model addresses a different ability. Use a supplied frame such as Это … only after it has been taught. Save the actual audio. Transcript correctness alone cannot establish hard/soft articulation or stress placement.
5. **Later return.** On another day, offer an identification or meaning-cued production opportunity before reopening the models. Keep help available. Begin with retained examples, then add genuinely new recordings and voices as available. Suggested next-day and later-week opportunities are engineering defaults, not validated intervals for these contrasts.

Four trials are deliberately a manageable opening, not a proficiency test. Do not require an accuracy streak to unlock lessons or conversation. Repeated difficulty should trigger comparison, a simpler explanation or a break, not an escalating queue. A smaller session or more replay is a present preference/support condition, not a diagnosis of working memory.

## Explanations that remain true

For the lateral contrast, a useful first cue is: **“Both start with an l sound. For soft л, raise the middle of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth while making the consonant. Listen to люк beside лук.”** The articulation is simultaneous; do not teach a compulsory extra full й syllable. Russian hard л is commonly velarized: “keep the tongue completely flat” and “hard means louder” are poor rules. English light/dark l can be an optional analogy, not a statement that an English allophone is the Russian target. Provide the audio contrast before loading the beginner with IPA or tongue diagrams.

For stress: **“The strong syllable changes: за́-мок is a castle; за-мо́к is a lock. Listen to the whole word.”** Stress involves duration, vowel quality and other cues; it is not simply higher pitch or extra volume. The unstressed vowels can sound different too. Do not mechanically move a pitch peak between otherwise identical synthetic syllables and call that natural Russian lexical stress. Acute marks are teaching annotations, usually absent from ordinary Russian text. They help specify the intended model but do not demonstrate that the learner can hear it.

Here лук is the onion sense; it can also mean a bow. That ambiguity belongs in optional detail rather than an added answer option. For later examples with ь, qualify the explanation: “Here ь marks soft л; it is not another syllable.” Do not generalize a single letter–sound cue into a universal spelling rule.

## What the research supports

| Primary evidence or synthesis | Finding and limits | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Lively, Logan & Pisoni 1993](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3509365/) | English /r–l/ training for Japanese listeners used identification, immediate feedback and varied natural tokens/talkers. Experiment 1 had six participants, five training talkers and 15 approximately 30-minute sessions. Its training vocabulary was separate from tests. This is a different contrast and a much larger dose than four Rufren trials. | Use varied examples and genuine held-out tests; do not describe two repeated clips as high-variability training. |
| [Lively et al. 1994](https://linguistics.berkeley.edu/~kjohnson/ling290e/lively_et_al_1994.pdf) | Nineteen Japanese trainees and an additional control group; English /r–l/ learning over three weeks was examined immediately and at three and six months. Generalization tests separated new words spoken by familiar versus unfamiliar talkers. | Distinguish token, lexical-context and voice generalization; include delayed observations. The study does not prescribe a Russian daily dose. |
| [Bradlow et al. 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3507383/) | Perceptual identification training improved production judged by native English listeners, including identification of intended minimal-pair members. | Perception can benefit production, but hearing success cannot replace a speaking sample or establish its quality. |
| [Roon, Kang & Whalen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1159/000505298) | Eighteen English-speaking learners without prior Slavic study received brief Russian palatalization familiarization. Both ultrasound and audio-only groups improved some outcomes; no general superiority of ultrasound was established. The nominal audio-only group also saw brief initial articulatory information. | Plain explanation plus useful sound models is a defensible starting point; advanced visualization is not a prerequisite. |
| [Hacking, Smith & Johnson 2017](https://eric-m-johnson.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/hacking-smith-johnson-2017.pdf) | EPG training of 10 Russian learners improved acoustic measures and modestly improved native-listener identification: 60% to 68%, t(9)=3.480, p<.01, §5.3/Table 5. | Retain both acoustic and communicative outcomes. This is not evidence for automatic grading of a beginner's lateral from a transcript. |
| [Chrabaszcz et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1044/2014_JSLHR-L-13-0279) | Forty-five English, Mandarin and Russian listeners identified stress in manipulated nonce words. Cue weighting differed across language groups; Russian listeners relied more on duration/intensity and little on pitch in this task. This was cue-perception research, not a Russian teaching intervention. | Do not teach a universal pitch-only stress detector. Use natural whole-word models; record which acoustic transformations are used. |
| [Uchihara, Karas & Thomson 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263125100879), [2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000195) | The perception synthesis covers 79 studies; the production synthesis covers 31 and finds small-to-medium gains with limited evidence for durable production transfer. Moderator associations do not isolate an optimal talker count or dose. | Add voices and contexts gradually, retain explicit production and delayed checks, and balance sound work with meaningful language. |

A source correction matters here: our earlier evidence note repeated Roon's characterization of Hacking's identification improvement as nonsignificant. The acquired Hacking primary paper reports the statistically significant result above. [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md) E6 now reflects the primary result. Its small sample and modest change still do not justify a broad mastery claim.

[Hayes-Harb & Barrios 2021](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444820000658) reviews Russian stress-mark and palatalization studies. Written knowledge can outpace auditory knowledge, and the reviewed stress-mark intervention did not resolve novice stress learning. That particular intervention was inspected through the review, not its unavailable primary full text. Text is useful teaching support; removing it for an auditory observation answers a different question. It is not a reason to ban Cyrillic from instruction.

## Cue fading and feedback are task decisions

Start with labelled models. During identification, preserve both meanings and remove only the selected member's revealing text. A first playback is the task input; extra playback is replay, and revealing the selected spelling/translation is answer-bearing support. Score first-play, replayed and revealed trials separately. On production, a preceding spoken model is target support, not task input merely because it is audio. Later meaning-cued production can precede that model without permanently disabling it.

Introduce a new voice or phonetic context after the first comparison is understandable; there is no need to master one fixed voice to perfection first. The starting number of voices, amount of blocking, and pace of support fading are testable product choices. A synthesizer's alternate voice is a new synthetic voice, not automatically an independent native-speaker sample. Generated audio needs content/contrast checks; provider success or an exact text match does not certify its phonetics. If only one token per category is available, teach with it and describe progress honestly as recognition of those examples.

Feedback should distinguish the selected category from a claim about *why* the learner erred. Choosing “onion” after hearing “hatch” does not prove the learner failed to raise the tongue or used a particular acoustic cue. Correct the answer, replay the contrast, and optionally offer one cue. For recorded production, preserve AI opinions with their actual audio access and scope; offer uncertainty and another model when no reliable acoustic assessment exists. Do not invent pronunciation precision from ASR output or generic model confidence.

## Data needed for a falsifiable transfer claim

A useful stimulus record needs the exact decoded-audio/content identity, word and intended meaning, contrast category, lexical stress, phonetic context/position, speaker or synthetic voice/model identity, recording/synthesis provenance, transformations and quality-check status. A new request ID or file hash alone does not establish new phonetic material; identical or nearly identical regenerated tokens remain effectively reused examples.

A useful trial record needs phase (labelled teaching, identification practice, assessment, imitation or meaning-cued production), task/stimulus IDs, candidate labels and positions, randomized order, playback/replay times, answer-bearing reveals, the first committed response and response time, feedback onset/content, and the actual production take where relevant. Preserve completion versus abandonment and audio playback failure. Latency on an interrupted device is not a pure perceptual measure.

Keep an exposure inventory across teaching, tests, previews and feedback. A pretest exposes its tokens too. Mark separately:

- **Same token:** a trained recording, possibly on another day.
- **New take:** an unheard recording of a familiar word by a familiar voice.
- **New voice:** an unheard voice producing familiar material.
- **New phonetic context/word:** an untrained environment, optionally crossed with a new voice.
- **Production transfer:** a fresh meaning-cued recording before a model, with judgment provenance and dimension.

Unknown exposure history must remain unknown. For a new word whose meaning has not been taught, a meaning-choice test confounds vocabulary with sound perception. Ask for the relevant sound category or stressed syllable instead, using previously taught response categories. Conversely, interpreting a familiar word from a new take is a legitimate lexical recognition test; it need not be mislabeled phonemic generalization.

For a later personal experiment, reserve tokens and voices before training and compare first responses on matched alternate sets at a declared delay, before remedial feedback. Keep acquisition time, feedback and available help comparable across training variants. Report each contrast and confusion direction, exact sample counts, support use, and trained versus untrained conditions. Ordinary practice can give immediate feedback; a small assessment block can defer it until the block ends, then teach normally. Four trials on two clips are too little to establish a stable generalization estimate, and repeated clips are correlated observations. Do not turn a short streak into a proficiency badge, a universal accuracy target or evidence of production improvement.
